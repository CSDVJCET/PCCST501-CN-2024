1. Network Management: SNMP, SMI & MIB-II

SNMP (Simple Network Management Protocol) provides an architecture to monitor and configure network devices:

SMI (Structure of Management Information)

Defines data types and rules for naming objects using hierarchical Object Identifiers (OIDs).

MIB-II (Management Information Base)

Hierarchical database of managed objects organized under iso(1).org(3).dod(6).internet(1).mgmt(2).mib-2(1).

SNMP Protocol & PDUs

Operates over UDP 161 (Polling) & UDP 162 (Traps). Uses GetRequest, GetNextRequest, SetRequest, and Trap PDUs.

2. Physical Layer: Nyquist & Shannon Channel Capacity

Nyquist Bit Rate (Noiseless Channel)
$$C = 2 \times B \times \log_2(L) \text{ bps}$$

Where $B$ is bandwidth in Hertz and $L$ is the number of discrete signal voltage levels.

Shannon Channel Capacity (Noisy Channel)
$$C = B \times \log_2(1 + \text{SNR}) \text{ bps}$$

Where $\text{SNR}$ is the linear Signal-to-Noise power ratio ($\text{SNR}_{dB} = 10 \log_{10}(\text{SNR})$).

3. Digital Transmission & Line Coding Schemes

Scheme Signal Behavior DC Component Self-Synchronization
NRZ-L (Level) Voltage level determines bit (0 = Positive, 1 = Negative) High DC Component Poor (fails on long strings of 0s/1s)
Manchester (IEEE 802.3) Mid-bit transition: 0 is Low-to-High, 1 is High-to-Low Zero DC (Balanced) Excellent (Guaranteed transition every bit)
Differential Manchester Always transition in middle. Inversion at start for 0, none for 1 Zero DC Excellent (Used in Token Ring)
AMI (Alternate Mark Inversion) 0 is Zero voltage; 1s alternate between $+V$ and $-V$ Zero DC Moderate (Requires scrambling like B8ZS/HDB3)
